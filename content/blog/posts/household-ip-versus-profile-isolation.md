A household IP is not a crime, and it is not isolation. Two authorized profiles on the same home connection will often share an exit. Platforms already expect that. Families share routers. Founders work from the same street as their personal life. A shared last mile does not prove the logins are one person in costume, and it does not prove the logins are cleanly split.

This note is not a proxy catalog. Proxy pages live elsewhere. Here the job is to say what a shared home exit does and does not prove, so you stop treating the street as either a verdict or an excuse.

This sits in [Social media](/blog/social-media).

## What a household exit actually is

The outbound IPv4 (and maybe IPv6) your ISP assigned to the router. Carrier-grade NAT in some regions. A laptop on coffee-shop Wi-Fi is not a household, even if it feels like one. A VPN on the whole PC is not a household either. It is a tunnel wrapping every profile plus personal Chrome.

[Multiple accounts on one PC](/blog/multiple-accounts-on-one-pc) is the desk map. [Authorized accounts only](/blog/authorized-accounts-only) still applies. A shared IP does not authorize a login you do not own.

## What it does not prove

It does not prove two profiles are isolated. Cookies, localStorage, pixels, and fingerprint records still can meet. [Cookie stores are not optional](/blog/cookie-jars-are-not-optional). [How accounts get linked](/blog/how-accounts-get-linked).

It does not prove two profiles are the same person cheating. Roommates, spouses, and a founder with a personal Facebook plus a company BM are ordinary.

It does not replace 2FA. [Two-factor stays in the real world](/blog/two-factor-stays-in-the-real-world).

| Signal | Shared household IP | Split cookie stores |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same street | Yes | Independent |
| Same login cookies | Only if you mixed them | No, if you split |
| Same pixel | Only if you mixed them | No, if you split |
| Same Windows user | Often yes | Still often yes |
| Proof of fraud | No | No |

## What I actually do at home

Personal Facebook and brand Business Manager: different profiles, same house IP. I do not buy a residential line just to feel professional on the brand login unless the platform already had a geo reason.

Client A and Client B: different profiles, often still the house IP. Platform roles for extra people. [Platform roles versus extra logins](/blog/platform-roles-versus-extra-logins). [Terms](/blog/multiple-accounts-and-platform-terms) if someone is asking "is two accounts on one IP allowed." Read the product terms. I am not your lawyer.

Do not clone a profile to "get a new IP." [Cloning a profile](/blog/cloning-a-profile-safely) copies cookies. A new street on old cookies is the proxy-plus-cookies failure, not a household trick.

## When the house IP is the wrong exit

The dashboard is geo-restricted to a country you are not in. The business already uses a still ISP line for that entity. You are on public Wi-Fi and you wanted a tunnel for the whole PC (that is a VPN job, and it wraps everything).

If you attach a proxy, attach it to the profile that needs it. Do not "fix" a cookie mix by changing the street.

## What I tell a founder who wants a second IP at home

Split the profiles first. If pixels still mix, you mixed cookies, not streets. A residential line will not unmix them. If the brand dashboard is geo-locked to another country, then buy the line for that profile only. Do not VPN the whole house and take personal banking with you.

Roommates on the same router running two authorized brands is ordinary. Two cloned cookie files on two laptops on that router is a linking story. [Cloning](/blog/cloning-a-profile-safely) is how that happens. Roles beat clones.

IPv6 can still show a second address. Check it. The "household IP" people argue about is often only the IPv4 they looked at once.

## FAQ

### Will Instagram ban me for personal plus brand on one home IP?

I will not predict enforcement. I will say the setup is ordinary. Split cookies. Use official roles. Do not mint extra graphs.

### Should I buy a proxy per authorized account at home?

Usually no. Split profiles first. Add a line when there is a geo or ASN reason.

### Does a shared IP mean I do not need an antidetect browser?

Opposite possible reading: you still need cookie splits when identities must not meet. The IP was never the split.
