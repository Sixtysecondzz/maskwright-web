They write create-unlimited guides. We write authorized only. If you do not have a right to the login, we do not have a page for that work.

That sentence is the whole policy. The rest of this note is what "authorized" means on a local Windows desk, and what it refuses. It is not a volume playbook with the volume words filed off.

This lives under [Social media](/blog/social-media).

## What authorized means

You registered the account. The legal entity owns it. You were invited as staff, partner, or page role. You moderate a community you were asked to moderate. You are looking at public pages without signing into someone else's session.

[Multiple accounts on one PC](/blog/multiple-accounts-on-one-pc) is the map for those logins. [Platform roles versus extra logins](/blog/platform-roles-versus-extra-logins) is how a second person gets in without becoming a second fake person. [Platform terms](/blog/multiple-accounts-and-platform-terms) is how to read extra-login language. Not legal advice.

| Example | Authorized? |
| --- | --- |
| Brand IG the company registered | Yes |
| BM you were invited into | Yes |
| Personal FB next to brand, both yours | Yes, split them |
| Staff login the client created for you | Yes |
| Extra seller ID to dodge a limit | No |
| Bought session / aged graph | No |

## What it refuses

Minting identities to dodge a cap. Buying Business Managers. Cookie dumps as onboarding. "Warmup" of a login you do not own. Keeping a disabled identity alive with a new fingerprint.

A cleaner canvas hash does not transfer ownership. [How accounts get linked](/blog/how-accounts-get-linked) still applies to authorized desks: pixels and cookies will link what you mix. [Cookie stores are not optional](/blog/cookie-jars-are-not-optional). Split the stores you have a right to split.

[Household IP](/blog/household-ip-versus-profile-isolation) is not a crime and not a loophole. [Gmail isolation](/blog/gmail-and-google-account-isolation) and [Telegram brand channels](/blog/telegram-brand-channels-on-a-local-profile) are platform-shaped versions of the same filter: if you should not open it, do not.

## How this shows up in the product

MaskWright will launch whatever profile you created. We do not have a server that checks your Business Manager invites. The policy is editorial and ethical, not a DRM. If you use the app to open logins you do not own, that is on you. We will not teach it.

No team seats, so we also will not sell a roster of extra identities as a feature. Invite people in the platform.

2FA stays on a device you hold. Do not paste backup codes into a profile folder.

## Gray cases I still say no to

A friend asked me to "just hold" a login they do not own. No. A marketplace ID opened after a policy hit, "only until appeal." No. A recycled Business Manager from a Telegram chat. No. A cookie dump as a contractor handoff. No: use a role.

Gray cases I say yes to: personal plus brand, both yours, split. Two legal entities you registered, split. Staff access the client created in your name, yes, in a profile that holds only that client.

If you need a lawyer, hire a lawyer. This page is the editorial door. MaskWright will still launch whatever you built. We will not write the missing how-to.

Public research without signing into someone else's account: yes. That is looking, not borrowing a session.

If a YouTube video used "authorized" to mean "I bought the cookie," that is not our definition. Purchase of a session is not an invite. Ownership sits with the person or entity the platform recognizes, plus staff they added. Everything else is a different industry. We do not write for it. Public research without someone else's session is still looking, and that stays allowed here.

## FAQ

### What if I manage five brands I was hired to run?

That is authorized. Split by client identity. Use platform roles for extra humans.

### What if the platform limited my ads account?

That is a business fact. A new profile is not a new business. Appeal or use official roles. Do not mint a shadow BM.

### Can you define authorized in one line?

A login you have a right to open: you own it, your entity owns it, or you were invited in.
