We dropped bandwidth-sharing SDKs as a product path. Some free browsers fund themselves by selling your last mile: a residential-looking exit out of your Windows box, rented to strangers. That model sometimes arrives bundled next to an antidetect drawer. We will not ship it.

This is a privacy note, not a pricing coupon. Free here means owned ad slots in chrome we draw. It does not mean your ISP line becomes inventory. The page lives under [Privacy](/blog/privacy) because the leftover is traffic you did not choose.

## What those SDKs actually do

They install a background client. Other people’s requests exit through your network. You might get a few dollars. They get a pool. Your ISP sees weird traffic. Your neighborhood IP picks up a reputation you did not earn. Consent UX is often a checkbox in an installer.

That is a cousin of the worst residential sourcing. It is also a cousin of malware-kit exits. I will not put it next to a product that holds Ads Manager cookies, even in a separate process.

## How we fund 0.1 instead

Dashboard ads. [What leaves this machine](/blog/what-leaves-this-machine) names the feed and the iframes. [Update channel](/blog/update-channel-habits) is packages, not a peer-to-peer mesh. No Honeygain-style module in the desktop repo.

[Cloud browsers](/blog/what-is-a-cloud-browser) and [cloud phones](/blog/what-is-a-cloud-phone) rent someone else’s machine. Bandwidth sharing rents yours. [Cloud browser risk](/blog/cloud-browser-risk) and [cloud phones versus local Windows](/blog/cloud-phones-versus-local-windows) are the other direction. [If a vendor is breached](/blog/if-a-vendor-is-breached) is worse if the vendor also routed stranger traffic through you.

[LocalStorage](/blog/local-storage-is-a-second-cookie) is still in your profile. Bandwidth sharing would have been in the whole OS path. Different layer, still a leftover I refuse.

| Funding idea | In MaskWright 0.1 |
| --- | --- |
| Seats / subscriptions | No |
| Bundled residential | No |
| Bandwidth-sharing SDK | No |
| Dashboard ad slots | Yes |
| Launch-tab hooks from ads JSON | Possible; named on the ads page |

I have watched “free antidetect” installers bundle a bandwidth client with a checkbox already ticked. The user wanted isolated cookies. They also became an exit for strangers. ISP abuse tickets follow. Neighborhood reputation follows. Consent was a skipped screen.

Our installer is NSIS for Windows x64. It may be unsigned. SmartScreen may warn. That is a different annoyance. It is not a silent last-mile SDK. If a fork of this app adds sharing, it is not the product I am describing.

Dashboard ads still use your connection to fetch creatives. That is you loading a page, not you proxying someone else’s crawl. Keep those sentences separate when a comment thread mashes them together.

Pushback I hear: “opt-in sharing could fund the app without ads.” It still turns the operator PC into inventory. Ads in chrome we draw are a page load. Bandwidth sharing is other people’s traffic on your ISP. I will keep the footer ad. I will not keep the SDK.

## FAQ

### Could you add it later as an opt-in?

I would still argue no. Opt-in SDKs in this category have a habit of becoming default. The product is a local launcher, not a last-mile marketplace.

### Do dashboard ads use my bandwidth the same way?

They load creatives in the operator UI. They do not turn your PC into someone else’s proxy. Different beast.

### Is the app still free?

Yes. [Download MaskWright](/download). Read the leave-the-machine list so “free” is not a mystery.

### Could ads in the dashboard use as much bandwidth as sharing?

No. Ads fetch creatives for you. Sharing would proxy other people’s sessions through your ISP. Different leftover. I will keep the footer slot. I will not keep the SDK.

If a fork of this app adds sharing, it is not the 0.1 product I am describing. Read the installer list twice.

We would rather show a footer ad than sell your line. If that trade feels worse than a $29 seat, buy a seated cloud product. Do not ask us to quietly become an exit.
