Media buying isolation is about keeping authorized ads accounts from sharing cookies. It is not a way around a spend cap. Competitor pages in this query still sell restore steps and recycled Business Managers. I will not. Limits are business facts. Profiles are for pixels and logins the desk already owns.

This commercial note lives in [Antidetect browsers](/blog/antidetect-browsers). Campaign Manager, Ads Manager, and pixel hygiene for each platform belong in the social and ecommerce clusters. The category question is here.

## Authorized buyers, official roles, then profiles

Ads accounts the business owns. Partner access you were invited into. A pixel that belongs to that brand. Those are authorized.

A second Business Manager you bought because the first one hit a spend limit is a terms and fraud problem. Facebook's account limits are business facts. A cleaner fingerprint does not raise a cap.

[What an antidetect browser is](/blog/what-is-an-antidetect-browser) is the isolation definition. [Custom Chromium versus system Chrome](/blog/custom-chromium-versus-system-chrome) is the engine caveat: if the patched core is missing, you are closer to stock Chrome than the homepage implies.

## What I split on a buying desk

Client A versus Client B. Different businesses, different pixels, different bills.

Personal Facebook versus client Ads Manager. The personal graph should not paint the client's ads login.

Research (Ads Library, public landing pages) versus the account that spends. [SEO research](/blog/antidetect-browsers-for-seo) stays cold. [Scraping](/blog/antidetect-browsers-for-web-scraping) is not a substitute for Ads Library.

Brand ads versus storefront admin can share if they are the same entity. [Dropshipping](/blog/antidetect-browsers-for-dropshipping) covers the shop split. [Affiliate](/blog/antidetect-browsers-for-affiliate-marketing) dashboards stay out of the ads profile if you do not want publisher cookies on the pixel.

| Login | Share with Ads Manager? |
| --- | --- |
| Same brand's pixel helper | Yes |
| Same brand's shop admin | Often yes |
| Other client's ads | No |
| Personal social | No |
| Public Ads Library research | Prefer a cold profile |

## Practical setup

One profile per authorized ads identity that must not share a pixel. Sticky HTTP or SOCKS5 if you attached a line. Do not rotate under a spend login. MaskWright does not bundle proxies.

2FA on a device you hold. Billing and tax tabs stay with the entity. Bulk start is for opening the day's authorized accounts, not for a grid of extra BMs.

Windows 10 and 11 x64, local store, no cloud phones. [Compare local versus cloud](/compare) if the team is arguing seats versus folders.

## What a buying week looks like

I sit in the client's Ads Manager after they invited the partner user. I do not keep that cookie in the same profile as my personal Facebook. I do not keep Client A's pixel helper in Client B's profile. Billing alerts stay in the client's profile. If spend paused, I read the account quality UI. I do not launch a second BM.

Ads Library research happens in a cold profile. I screenshot and take notes. I do not import those cookies into the spend login. Window-sync and RPA are not in MaskWright 0.1, and I do not want them driving a live ads account.

If the team wanted seats so three buyers share one vault, that is a hosted product. We will not fake an audit trail. Ads Manager already logs who changed a campaign.

## FAQ

### Will a new profile lift an ads spending limit?

No. Limits are account and business facts. A new profile is not a new business.

### Should every campaign get its own profile?

No. Campaigns live inside Ads Manager. Split by authorized identity and pixel, not by campaign name.

### Can contractors share one MaskWright profile?

They should not share a folder. Invite them in Business Manager. Mailing user-data is mailing the cookie store.
