Shared subnets mean you inherit neighbors. Residential products sell a household-looking address. That address usually sits on a /24 or a small set of prefixes that many customers have already used. Tired space stays tired when you attach it to a local profile. Vendor blogs skip this because it makes the pool look worse. This note does not skip it.

[Proxies in an antidetect browser](/blog/proxies-in-an-antidetect-browser) is the pillar. The rest of the cluster lives under [Proxies](/blog/proxies). We [do not sell residential](/blog/residential-proxies-we-do-not-sell) exits. Authorized work only.

## What "shared" actually means

A residential exit is an IP assigned in a way that geo databases call a household or a similar ISP customer. It is not a private driveway. Carrier and ISP networks put many subscribers behind one prefix. Proxy products put many customers behind one prefix on purpose. You are not the only person who has asked that /24 to open a login this month.

Neighbors include other operators, other tools, and whatever got that prefix listed. You do not get a clean history by paying for the label residential. You get an address that looks like a house and behaves like a bus stop if the prefix is famous.

[How to test a residential exit](/blog/how-to-test-a-residential-exit) is attach, place, type, stick, leaks. This page is the reputation row that ip-api.com will not print.

| Kind of share | Who you sit with | What you inherit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ISP /24 | Real subscribers plus pools | Mixed reputation |
| Proxy product prefix | Other customers of that SKU | Pool history |
| CGNAT mobile address | Carrier subscribers | Shared IPv4 by design |
| VPN prefix | VPN users | Usually labeled as VPN |

## Tired is a history, not a score

I will not publish a "bad ASN" list. Lists rot and they get used as hide-the-pool guides. Tired means: the prefix has been in a lot of pools, the sticky IP you drew has already been through many user-agents, or the provider's whole /24 is a known product.

You notice tired space when a site that was fine on the house path suddenly challenges a coherent profile, or when the same prefix shows up in two "different" providers. You also notice it when the ISP label says residential and every operator you know has that /24 in a screenshot.

[Residential versus a VPN](/blog/residential-versus-vpn) is a type cut. A VPN prefix can be tired too. The difference is honesty. A VPN is a VPN. A residential SKU on a prefix that only exists to be sold is a labeling problem. [Ethics](/blog/residential-proxy-ethics) is how the address was assigned. Shared and unethical can both be true. Shared and consented can both be true. Do not flatten those.

## What a local profile inherits

The app isolates cookies. It does not isolate prefixes. Two MaskWright profiles with two sticky usernames can still land on the same /24. They will not share cookies. They may share a reputation they did not earn together.

A [mobile](/blog/what-are-mobile-proxies) prefix is shared in a different way: CGNAT, many subscribers, one address. That is not the same as a residential proxy pool, but the neighbor problem rhymes. [A mobile ASN on a desktop browser](/blog/mobile-asn-on-a-desktop-browser) is the coherence mismatch. [Mobile proxy versus a cloud phone](/blog/mobile-proxy-versus-cloud-phone) is the product mismatch. Neither cures a tired residential /24.

The index that stores your line is `profiles.json`, AES-256-GCM at rest. Encryption does not wash the street. Cookie JSON on disk is still plaintext. The site sees the IP, not the vault.

## What I do with tired space

I test before the login. If the prefix is one I already know as a pool, I decide whether this authorized profile can live with that. A public research fetch you already have a right to run is a different job than a Business Manager you intend to keep for years.

I do not warm a subnet. I do not bounce user-agents on a tired IP to see if the challenge goes away. That teaches the site a circus.

I do not move a live login onto a new prefix because the old one got tired. A new exit does not clean an old cookie. If the profile must move, that is a new device story you chose with your eyes open, not a tactic I will optimize.

I do not ask MaskWright to broker a cleaner pool. We will not. Bring another line, or use the house path.

## FAQ

**Does a clean fingerprint cancel a famous /24?**
No. The site sees the IP.

**Can two profiles share a /24 safely?**
They will not share cookies. They may share reputation. Decide if that is acceptable for those accounts.

**Will you list bad ASNs?**
No. Lists rot and become hide guides.

Shared residential space is normal. Surprise is not. Name the prefix, name the neighbors you can see, and only then open work you already have a right to open.
