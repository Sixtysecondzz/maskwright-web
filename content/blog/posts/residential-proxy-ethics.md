Residential is a claim about how an IP looks, not a receipt that a person agreed to carry your traffic. Cheap pools skip that sentence. Vendor blogs skip it because ethics do not convert. I will not skip it.

A residential exit is supposed to be an address assigned to a household or a similar access network. [What residential proxies are](/blog/what-are-residential-proxies) is the definition. This page is the consent page. The ASN can look fine while the host is a compromised box, a silent “earn money” app, or a person who ticked a box they did not understand.

## Consent is the first filter

Ask how the provider sources the network. SDK-in-an-app, ISP partnership, and “users opted in to share unused bandwidth” are different stories. The last one is where malware-adjacent kits hide. If the FAQ cannot name a sourcing model without a metaphor, I treat the pool as untrustworthy for any login I care about.

I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice. It is operator hygiene. I will not attach a mystery residential line to Seller Central, Ads Manager, or a bank. Research on public pages is the only place I will even consider a pool I cannot explain.

[How to test a residential exit](/blog/how-to-test-a-residential-exit) is IP, geo, and whether the session stays put. Testing does not prove consent. A clean ip-api.com result can still be a hijacked last mile.

## What “residential” is not

It is not a VPN. [Residential versus VPN](/blog/residential-versus-vpn) is the split. A VPN is usually one provider ASN you chose. Residential is supposed to look like someone else’s house. That look is exactly why consent matters more, not less.

It is not datacenter. [Residential versus datacenter](/blog/residential-versus-datacenter-proxies) is reputation and price. Datacenter is often cleaner to explain (a server you rent) and noisier on sites that hate hosting ranges.

It is not automatically better than ISP. [ISP proxies on a local profile](/blog/isp-proxies-on-a-local-profile) are often a named circuit with a contract. I prefer a boring ISP sticky over a huge residential pool I cannot audit.

[Shared residential subnets](/blog/shared-residential-subnets) are how a “unique” exit still sits next to a hundred other customers. Ethics and uniqueness are different problems.

## FAQ

### Is every cheap residential pool stolen?

No. Some are badly explained ISP leftovers. Some are real opt-in apps. Some are junk. Price is a hint, not a verdict. If you cannot get a sourcing answer in writing, I would not put a password behind it.

### Can MaskWright tell me if an exit is ethical?

No. We can check connectivity and geo when you ask. We cannot see how that household was enrolled. That is your provider conversation.

### Should I use residential for everything?

Usually no. Datacenter or ISP is enough for a lot of public research. Save residential for jobs that actually need a household ASN, and only from a provider you can defend.
