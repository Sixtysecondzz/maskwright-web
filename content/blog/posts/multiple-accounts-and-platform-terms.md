This is a terms of service note, not legal advice. I am not your counsel. I will not restate any platform's contract as if I signed it for you. I will tell you how to read extra-login language before you treat a second profile as a loophole.

No evasion framing. If the question is "are multiple accounts legal," the honest answer starts with: legal in which country, under which statute, versus allowed under which product terms. Those are different questions. A browser cannot answer either of them. A local profile also cannot.

This sits in [Social media](/blog/social-media).

## Terms are not a browser feature

Platforms write extra-account rules in Help Centers that change. Some allow one personal and many Pages. Some allow multiple Business Managers with conditions. Some forbid circumventing limits with extra identities. I will not quote a 2024 help article as if it were still live. Open the current policy for the product you use.

[Authorized accounts only](/blog/authorized-accounts-only) is our filter regardless of how clever a fingerprint is. [Multiple accounts on one PC](/blog/multiple-accounts-on-one-pc) assumes that filter. [Platform roles versus extra logins](/blog/platform-roles-versus-extra-logins) is the setup platforms already sold you.

## How I read extra-login language

I look for whether the platform distinguishes **Pages / shops / properties** (often many, under one person) from **user identities** (often one personal graph). I look for **Business / partner** programs that exist so a second human does not need a second fake person. I look for **limit circumvention** language: if they already said do not mint extra accounts to dodge a cap, a new profile is not a loophole.

[Household IP](/blog/household-ip-versus-profile-isolation) is usually not the clause. Shared home exits are ordinary. Cookie mixing is how you create a terms problem you did not need. [How accounts get linked](/blog/how-accounts-get-linked).

[Cloning a profile](/blog/cloning-a-profile-safely) copies the session. That can violate "do not share your password" even when both humans are authorized. Use roles.

[First profile checklist](/blog/first-profile-checklist) if you are setting up an authorized login and you wanted a practical list, not a contract.

## What I will not write

How to stay under a detection threshold. How many accounts per IP. How to keep a disabled login alive. How to read a ban as a puzzle. [Two-factor](/blog/two-factor-stays-in-the-real-world) stays on a device you hold because the terms also expect a real person.

If you are in a dispute, screenshot the current terms, talk to the platform, talk to a lawyer. Do not ask a local Chromium to be your defense.

## What I do when a client asks "is this allowed?"

I send them the live Help Center URL, not a screenshot from a course. I ask whether they mean a second Page, a second user identity, or a second person on the same Page. Those three are different sentences in most products. I recommend roles for the third. I recommend authorized split profiles for personal versus brand when both are theirs. I refuse to design extra identities to dodge a cap.

If they are already in a review with the platform, I tell them to talk to the platform and a lawyer. I do not tune fingerprints as a defense. [Two-factor](/blog/two-factor-stays-in-the-real-world) and staff logs help more than a new canvas seed.

I also will not translate a blog's "accounts per IP" table into a setup. Those tables are volume playbooks. Household IP plus split cookies is ordinary. Cloned cookies plus hoppy proxies is not a terms strategy.
