Multilogin positions itself as cloud phones for social media accounts. MaskWright is a local Windows app that stores profiles on your disk. Both tools solve account isolation. But they target different users with different architectures.

This is not a feature badge comparison. I am going to walk through what cloud phones actually mean, when they make sense, and when a local Windows tool is the better fit.

## The core difference: cloud phones versus local profiles

Multilogin runs your accounts on cloud phones. These are virtual mobile or desktop environments hosted on their infrastructure. You access them remotely. Your sessions live on their servers.

MaskWright runs your accounts on your Windows PC. Profiles are stored locally, encrypted, in a folder you can point at. You launch them directly. Your sessions live on your hardware.

This architecture difference drives everything else: pricing, access, team features, and control.

## What Multilogin offers

Multilogin built for social media marketers and agencies. Their positioning is clear from their homepage: cloud phones for social accounts.

- Target users: Social media marketers, creators, agencies
- Platforms: Cloud-first (access from any device)

- Team features: Yes, with workspace sharing and permissions

- Mobile simulation: Yes, cloud phones emulate mobile devices

## What MaskWright offers

MaskWright built for Windows operators who want local control.

**Verified facts about MaskWright:**

- Architecture: Local Windows app
- Target users: Windows operators, solo users, privacy-focused users
- Platforms: Windows 10/11 only
- Pricing: Free, no profile limits
- Team features: No (by design, requires server)
- Built-in proxies: No (bring your own HTTP or SOCKS5)
- Mobile simulation: No (desktop fingerprints only)
- Support: Documentation and community

## When cloud phones make sense

Multilogin is the right tool if you need:

**Mobile account management.** Cloud phones emulate real mobile devices. This matters for platforms like TikTok, Instagram, and Snapchat that treat mobile traffic differently from desktop.

**Cross-device access.** You launch profiles from your laptop, desktop, or borrowed machines. The session follows you because it lives in the cloud.

**Team collaboration.** You share profiles with team members, set permissions, and manage access levels. Multilogin supports unlimited team members on Business plans.

**Built-in infrastructure.** You want proxies, cloud phones, and browser profiles in one subscription. No need to source proxies separately.

**Social media at scale.** You manage dozens or hundreds of social accounts for clients. Cloud phones let you scale without buying hardware.

**Zero local setup.** You do not want to install software or manage local storage. Everything runs in the cloud.

None of this is a weakness. Cloud architecture solves real problems for social media agencies and cross-platform teams.

## When local profiles make sense

MaskWright is the right tool if you want:

**Local storage.** Your profiles live on your disk, encrypted, in a folder you control. You can back it up. You can move it. You know exactly where it is.

**No account requirement.** MaskWright opens when you click it. No login, no verification, no landlord.

**No profile limits.** You are limited only by your disk space and RAM. No subscription tiers. No per-profile pricing.

**Windows-first focus.** You run Windows 10/11 and do not need cross-platform access. You do not need mobile emulation.

**Bring your own proxies.** You already source residential or ISP proxies. You do not need a vendor marketplace.

**No second copy of sessions.** You do not want a vendor holding your account sessions in the cloud. If Multilogin has a breach, outage, or policy change, your access is affected. With MaskWright, your profiles are on your PC.

**Free software.** You do not want a monthly bill for profile management.

These are not oversights. They are design choices for operators who want local control.

## The honest take on cloud phones

Cloud phones are a real innovation for social media management. If you run TikTok, Instagram, or Snapchat accounts at scale, mobile emulation matters. Platforms treat mobile traffic differently. Cloud phones let you manage mobile accounts without buying dozens of physical phones.

But cloud phones come with trade-offs:

**You pay for convenience.** Multilogin charges subscription fees. At scale (50-100+ profiles), this is a significant monthly cost.

**You trust a vendor.** Your sessions live on their servers. If they have a breach, outage, or policy change, your access is affected.

**You lose local control.** You cannot back up a cloud phone to your local drive. You cannot move it to another provider. You are locked into their ecosystem.

**You may not need mobile.** If you manage Facebook ads, Google Ads, Amazon seller accounts, or ecommerce stores, desktop profiles are often sufficient. Mobile emulation is not required.

## The honest take on local profiles

Local profiles give you control. Your sessions live on your hardware. You back them up. You move them. You know where they are.

But local profiles come with trade-offs:

**No cross-device access.** You launch profiles only from this PC. If you need to work from a different machine, you cannot access your profiles.

**No team features.** MaskWright does not have user management or permission systems. If you need to share a profile, you export cookies and import them on another machine.

**No mobile emulation.** MaskWright emulates desktop fingerprints only. If you need mobile accounts, you need a different tool.

**No built-in proxies.** You source your own HTTP or SOCKS5 proxies. This adds complexity but also flexibility.

**Windows only.** MaskWright does not run on macOS or Linux.

**Use MaskWright if:**

- You run Windows 10/11 only
- You manage desktop accounts (Facebook ads, Google Ads, Amazon, ecommerce)
- You want profiles stored locally on your PC
- You do not want an account to launch the tool
- You already source your own proxies
- You want a free app with no profile limits
- You do not want a vendor holding a second copy of your sessions

## Making the switch

If you are considering switching from Multilogin to MaskWright:

**Understand what you lose.** You lose mobile emulation. You lose cross-device access. You lose team features. Make sure you do not need these before you switch.

**Export your cookies.** Multilogin lets you export cookies from profiles. You can import these into MaskWright.

**Recreate fingerprint settings.** You will need to manually recreate desktop fingerprint configurations in MaskWright.

**Test before migrating work.** Launch the new profiles, check your exit IP, verify timezone matches your proxy, and confirm sessions work before moving real accounts.

## FAQ

### Is Multilogin better than MaskWright?

Neither is universally better. Multilogin offers cloud phones with mobile emulation, team features, and cross-device access. MaskWright offers local storage, no account requirement, and free unlimited profiles. Your needs determine which fits.

### Can I migrate from Multilogin to MaskWright?

Yes, but not automatically. Export cookies from Multilogin profiles and import them into MaskWright. You will need to recreate fingerprint settings manually. Mobile profiles cannot be migrated to desktop-only profiles.

### Does MaskWright support mobile profiles?

No. MaskWright emulates desktop Windows fingerprints only. If you need mobile emulation for TikTok, Instagram, or Snapchat, use a cloud phone tool like Multilogin.

### Why does MaskWright not have team features?

Team features require a server to manage users, permissions, and audit logs. MaskWright is a local app with no server component. If you need team access, export cookies and share them through your own secure channels, or use a cloud tool designed for teams.

### Is Multilogin worth the cost?

If you need cloud phones, mobile emulation, and team features, yes. Multilogin solves real problems for social media agencies. If you only need desktop profiles and work solo on Windows, a local tool like MaskWright may be more cost-effective.

## Final word

Multilogin built a cloud phone platform for social media marketers. MaskWright built a local Windows app for operators who want control. Both products are legitimate. Both solve real problems.

The question is not which is better. The question is whether you need cloud phones with mobile emulation and team features, or whether you want local profiles on your Windows PC with no subscription and no vendor lock-in.

If you need cloud phones, Multilogin is the tool. If you want local profiles, MaskWright is the tool.
