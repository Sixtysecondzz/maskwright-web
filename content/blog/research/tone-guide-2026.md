# Tone guide (2026)

Source we studied: https://gologin.com/blog/multilogin-chrome-browser/

Do not copy that article. Do not copy its structure, jokes, promo codes, or "without bans" CTAs. Steal the *voice qualities* only.

## What that piece does well

- Talks like a person who sat down and used the thing.
- Puts the useful caveat in the first screen ("one thing worth flagging").
- Short sections. Plain H2s. A TLDR or a table when comparing.
- Specific details: OS version, live chat, a setting that was missing, a checker result.
- Direct opinions: expensive, sparse, outdated, worth testing yourself.
- FAQ that answers the search query in normal English.
- Invites the reader to check, not to join a religion.

## What our current posts do wrong

- Too many metaphors: workshop, jar, room, locker, costume, bench, filing system.
- Opens like a manifesto instead of a useful note.
- Repeats "this bench will not" and "authorized rooms" until it sounds like a template.
- Sounds like a founder essay, not a 2026 blog post someone would finish.

## New voice

Write like a competent operator explaining something to another competent operator, in a review or how-to, not a sermon.

- First person is normal: I, we, I noticed, I keep, I do not.
- Concrete first. Metaphor only if it earns one sentence.
- Flag the useful fact in paragraph one.
- Use a table when you are comparing two options.
- Use a short FAQ when the search query has leftover questions.
- Say when something is unnecessary, overpriced, or misunderstood.
- Invite the reader to verify. Do not invent test scores we did not run.

## Hard no

- No em dashes or en dashes.
- No "In today's digital landscape" or "Whether you're a".
- No "Download and manage accounts without bans."
- No farm, warmup, or ban-evasion playbooks.
- No invented MaskWright features. Read product-capabilities.md.
- No copying GoLogin wording, headings, or pricing tables.
- Do not turn every article into a Multilogin hit piece. Most posts are not reviews.

## Keep

- Exact frontmatter from the existing file (slug, title, meta, author, published, category, tags, related).
- 700-1000 words standard. Pillars 1500-2200 if the subject needs it.
- 4-8 contextual internal links to related slugs.
- One hub link: /blog/{category}
- Product honesty: profiles.json is encrypted, Chromium user-data dirs are not. No seats. No cloud phones. No Playwright product. BYO HTTP/SOCKS5.

## Rewrite the whole body

Replace the body. Do not lightly edit a few sentences. New opening, new section titles, new examples. Same search intent. Same slug.
